### [BLOCKER] The 7.4 V pyro rail from P1O.2 to P2O/P3O/P4O (including the chute) has 43% of the copper cross-section the design's own criterion requires, because every sizing tool assumes 1 oz copper on the 0.5 oz inner layers.

- **category:** current-capacity/thermal
- **location:** c:\Users\braxt\VS Code Projects\TVC PID Research\PCBs\Impulse_2.3_kicad\Impulse_2.3.kicad_pcb (net "7.4V", In1.Cu 2.00 mm and In2.Cu 1.40 mm runs at y~41.2-41.5, x 138.598-166.792); PCBs/Impulse_2.3_kicad/_current_budget.py:2; PCBs/Impulse_2.3_kicad/fab/ORDER_NOTES.txt ("WHERE PARALLEL CONDUCTORS EXIST" and "RESULT -- firing loop at 18 A" blocks)
- **evidence:** Board stackup in c:\Users\braxt\VS Code Projects\TVC PID Research\PCBs\Impulse_2.3_kicad\Impulse_2.3.kicad_pcb declares In1.Cu/In2.Cu thickness 0.0152 mm against F.Cu/B.Cu 0.035 mm (43.4%).

Geometry (raw segments from the same file, net "7.4V"): the F.Cu 2.50 mm rail from Q21.5 terminates at P1O.2. From there the ONLY conductors to P2O.2/P3O.2/P4O.2 are a daisy chain of In1.Cu 2.00 mm (3 hops x 9.40 mm) paralleled by In2.Cu 1.40 mm (3 hops x ~8.1 mm). A max-flow/min-cut over the net's copper (pads bonded by rectangle overlap, T-junctions split, vias as pi*d*25um) gives the cut as exactly those two conductors.

Cross-section: 2.00*0.0152 + 1.40*0.0152 = 0.0517 mm^2. The project's own criterion (PCBs/Impulse_2.3_kicad/_current_budget.py, 18 A / 900 ms / <=100 C adiabatic) demands 3.44 mm of 1 oz = 0.1204 mm^2. That is 43%, i.e. an outer-equivalent width of 1.48 mm, not the 3.4 mm claimed.

Using _current_budget.py's own constants (rho 1.72e-8, cv 3.45e6): adiabatic rise 544 C at 18 A over 900 ms, and 284 C even at the fully-decayed 13 A. Onderdonk fusing current for a 0.9 s pulse = 15.7 A, inside the stated 13-18 A operating band. At the 200 ms basis ORDER_NOTES quotes, it is 121 C, not the ~23 C in its RESULT table.

Root cause is a single thickness constant: _current_budget.py line 2 is `CU_RHO=1.72e-8; CU_CV=3.45e6; T=35e-6      # 1 oz outer`, and _size_by_current.py / _uniform_runs.py reason in width only, with no per-layer term anywhere (grep for 0.035/oz/thick across all board scripts hits only that one line and a comment). fab/ORDER_NOTES.txt states it explicitly: "Q21 -> P2/P3/P4  reads 1.4 mm on In2, PARALLELED by 2.0 mm on In1 -> 3.4 mm effective" and "distribution rail to P2-P4  3.4 mm eff (In2 1.4 || In1 2.0)  ~23 C" -- adding widths across layers of different copper weight. The same error appears in "a 0.6 mm via's barrel is ~0.024 mm^2 against 0.07 mm^2 for a 2 mm trace" (a 2 mm INNER trace is 0.0304 mm^2, only 1.3x a via).

Why it lands on recovery: Firmware/Ascent_TVC/Ascent_TVC.ino:201 sets PYRO_CHUTE = P4, the far end of the chain (3 hops, ~28 mm, 9.4 mOhm cold). PYRO_ASCENT = P3 (line 200) fires first through two of the same three conductors, so the ascent firing pre-stresses the chute's feed and nothing re-checks continuity between firings.

By contrast the four drain legs, which the same current passes through, were correctly sized: 3.40 mm B.Cu x 4.88 mm = 0.1190 mm^2 = 99% of criterion, 103 C. So the high side of one series loop has 2.3x less copper than the low side.

Caveat, stated honestly: adiabatic is a conservative upper bound and ignores the planes 0.21 mm away. But the comparison is internal -- same current, same board, same heat-sinking assumptions -- so the 2.3x cross-section asymmetry (5.3x in power density) holds regardless of the absolute derating.
- **fix:** Add the layer-thickness term to _current_budget.py / _size_by_current.py / _uniform_runs.py (T = 0.035 outer, 0.0152 inner) and re-run the sizing; the 2.5 mm floor for a 7.4V run must become ~5.7 mm on an inner layer to be equivalent. Then either (a) route the distribution rail on F.Cu or B.Cu at 3.4 mm like the drain legs, (b) add a third and fourth parallel conductor (F.Cu and B.Cu runs alongside the existing In1/In2 pair) to reach 0.12 mm^2, or (c) feed each connector's pin 2 from Q21 individually rather than daisy-chaining, so no hop carries another channel's current. Correct the two ORDER_NOTES claims that add widths across layers, and re-check the "worst leg ~139 C adiabatic" statement in the "DO NOT SHORTEN THE 900 ms" paragraph -- the worst leg is 544 C by this measurement.

### [MAJOR] FAILURE_MODES B7 is marked green on a diagnostic the sense circuit cannot provide: the LED clamp behind a 10 k resistor cannot grade joint quality, so a joint bad enough to kill the channel reads healthy.

- **category:** failure-mode-mismatch
- **location:** c:\Users\braxt\VS Code Projects\TVC PID Research\FAILURE_MODES.md:29 (row B7); circuit: PCBs\Impulse_2.3_kicad\Impulse_2.3.kicad_sch, R37/R41/R42/R43 10 k plus LEDs P1-P4
- **evidence:** FAILURE_MODES.md line 29 (B7, status green) justifies itself as: "printPyroStatus() now prints all four channels with raw sense volts, live at ARM (2 s cadence, so you can re-crimp and watch it change)", and interprets "a channel at 0.95 V" as "a joint about to open in flight."

The circuit cannot do that. The sense node is a red LED clamp fed from the 7.4 V rail through the initiator and R37/R41/R42/R43 = 10 k. Joint resistance changes the LED current by -R_joint/10k, and the LED converts that to a Vf shift of n*Vt*ln(I/I0). With n = 1.8 and a 12-bit 3.3 V ADC (1 LSB = 0.806 mV):

  R_joint =    10 ohm -> -0.10% current -> -0.05 mV  = -0.1 LSB
  R_joint =   100 ohm -> -0.99% current -> -0.46 mV  = -0.6 LSB
  R_joint =  1000 ohm -> -9.1%  current -> -4.43 mV  = -5.5 LSB
  R_joint = 10000 ohm -> -50%   current -> -32.3 mV  = -40 LSB

A 100 ohm joint would drop the firing current from 18 A to 0.073 A -- a channel that cannot fire -- and it moves the reading by 0.46 mV, which is under one LSB and roughly a quarter of what a 1 C temperature change does to the LED (-2 mV/C). Even a 1 kohm joint is swamped by a 2.5 C shift. The circuit only responds once the joint approaches 10 kohm, by which point it is an open circuit for every purpose.

So intermediate sense voltages do not indicate marginal joints. They indicate the floating open node -- which is exactly what the firmware found and what forced PYRO_CONT_V from 0.9 to 1.5 V. B7's own reference reading of "0.95 V" corresponds to an LED passing microamps, i.e. a megohm-scale joint, not one "about to open."

The measurement direction B7 claims (watch the volts change as you re-crimp) is therefore not available on this hardware, so the green status is not earned. B3 ("Pyro commanded but wire never burns", yellow) is unaffected -- post-fire continuity really is a binary open/closed question and the circuit answers that one correctly.
- **fix:** Downgrade B7 to yellow and restate what the circuit actually detects: open vs not-open, with an effective trip around 10 kohm of joint resistance. If joint quality genuinely needs measuring, it needs a different circuit -- e.g. a low-side current source through the initiator with a sub-100 mA sense, or a four-wire measurement at the connector -- not a smaller LED resistor, since dropping R37 far enough to resolve 100 ohm would push the sense current toward initiator no-fire territory. The cheap interim is procedural: measure each initiator's resistance with a meter before fitting, which is what the 0.30 ohm figure in ORDER_NOTES came from anyway.

### CONFIRMED — Docs state 2.9 mOhm as GUARANTEED at VGS=3V for the CSD17301Q5A; the datasheet's guaranteed (max) value in that row is 3.7 mOhm

Confirmed against the primary source. I fetched the TI datasheet PDF from ti.com/lit/gpn/CSD17301Q5A and extracted the text directly rather than relying on a search snippet.

ELECTRICAL CHARACTERISTICS table, column header line: 'PARAMETER TEST CONDITIONS MIN TYP MAX UNIT'. The RDS(on) rows extract as:
  'VGS = 3V, ID = 25A            2.9  3.7  mOhm'
  'RDS(on) Drain to Source On Resistance  VGS = 4.5V, ID = 25A  2.3  3  mOhm'
  'VGS = 8V, ID = 25A            2    2.6  mOhm'
MIN is blank in all three, so the pair is TYP then MAX. 2.9 mOhm at VGS=3V is the TYPICAL; 3.7 mOhm is the guaranteed maximum. The finding's 4.5 V (2.3/3.0) and 8 V (2.0/2.6) figures are also exact.
